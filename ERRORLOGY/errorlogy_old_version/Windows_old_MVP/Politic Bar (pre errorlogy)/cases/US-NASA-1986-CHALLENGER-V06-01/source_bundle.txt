SOURCE 1 — Report of the Presidential Commission on the Space Shuttle
Challenger Accident (a.k.a. "Rogers Commission Report"), Vol. I, Chapter 4:
"The Cause of the Accident" and related findings on the decision to launch.
Published June 6, 1986. URL: https://history.nasa.gov/rogersrep/v1ch4.htm
==============================================================================

From Vol. I, Chapter V, "The Contributing Cause of the Accident", pp. 82–
104 (decision chain reconstruction):

"The decision to launch the Challenger was flawed. Those who made that
decision were unaware of the recent history of problems concerning the
O-rings and the joint and were unaware of the initial written
recommendation of the contractor advising against the launch at
temperatures below 53 degrees Fahrenheit and the continuing opposition
of the engineers at Thiokol after the management reversed its position.
They did not have a clear understanding of Rockwell's concern that it
was not safe to launch because of ice on the pad. If the decisionmakers
had known all of the facts, it is highly unlikely that they would have
decided to launch 51-L on January 28, 1986."

Sequence of events on the L-1 teleconference, night of January 27, 1986:

"At approximately 5:45 pm EST on January 27, 1986, Mr. Allan McDonald
requested that Mr. Joe Kilminster [Thiokol VP, Space Booster Programs]
contact Mr. Larry Mulloy [NASA Marshall, Solid Rocket Booster Project
Manager] by teleconference... During the teleconference, Mr. Roger
Boisjoly [Thiokol engineer] presented charts showing the correlation
between O-ring blow-by and cold weather, citing the January 1985
STS-51C flight (launch temperature 53°F) which had experienced the
worst blow-by observed to that date."

On the management caucus and reversal:

"Mr. Jerald Mason [Thiokol Senior VP, Wasatch Division] turned to Mr.
Robert Lund [Thiokol VP Engineering] and said, 'Take off your
engineering hat and put on your management hat.' Following a roughly
30-minute off-line caucus, Thiokol management returned to the
teleconference and recommended launch. Mr. Mulloy had earlier remarked,
'My God, Thiokol, when do you want me to launch, next April?'"

Commission finding on Level III suppression:

"The Commission is troubled by what appears to be a propensity of
management at Marshall to contain potentially serious problems and to
attempt to resolve them internally rather than communicate them forward.
This tendency is altogether at odds with the need for Marshall to function
as part of a system working toward successful flight missions, interfacing
and communicating with the other parts of the system that work to the
same end."

Key finding 1 of Chapter V:

"The Commission concluded that there was a serious flaw in the decision
making process leading up to the launch of flight 51-L. A well-structured
and managed system emphasizing safety would have flagged the rising
doubts about the Solid Rocket Booster joint seal."

SOURCE 2 — Rogers Commission Report, Vol. II, Appendix F: "Personal
Observations on the Reliability of the Shuttle" by R. P. Feynman.
URL: https://history.nasa.gov/rogersrep/v2appf.htm
==============================================================================

Feynman's estimates of failure probability, Appendix F, §"Official
Estimates", para 3:

"It appears that there are enormous differences of opinion as to the
probability of a failure with loss of vehicle and of human life. The
estimates range from roughly 1 in 100 to 1 in 100,000. The higher figures
come from the working engineers, and the very low figures from management.
What are the causes and consequences of this lack of agreement? Since
1 part in 100,000 would imply that one could put a Shuttle up each day
for 300 years expecting to lose only one, we could properly ask 'What is
the cause of management's fantastic faith in the machinery?'"

On the SRB seals specifically:

"The O-rings of the Solid Rocket Boosters were not designed to erode.
Erosion was a clue that something was wrong. Erosion was not something
from which safety could be inferred."

Closing paragraph:

"For a successful technology, reality must take precedence over public
relations, for nature cannot be fooled."

SOURCE 3 — "Investigation of the Challenger Accident: Report of the
Committee on Science and Technology", U.S. House of Representatives,
99th Congress, 2d Session, House Report 99-1016, October 29, 1986.
GPO: https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/GPO-CRPT-99hrpt1016
==============================================================================

From the Committee's conclusions, pp. 4–6:

"The Committee feels that the underlying problem which led to the
Challenger accident was not poor communication or underlying procedures
as implied by the Rogers Commission conclusion. Rather, the fundamental
problem was poor technical decision-making over a period of several years
by top NASA and contractor personnel, who failed to act decisively to
solve the increasingly serious anomalies in the Solid Rocket Booster
joints."

On the role of schedule pressure:

"Meeting flight schedules and cutting costs were given a higher priority
than flight safety."

SOURCE 4 — Testimony of Roger M. Boisjoly before the Presidential
Commission, February 25, 1986. Commission Vol. IV.
URL: https://history.nasa.gov/rogersrep/v4index.htm
==============================================================================

Boisjoly on the teleconference reversal (Commission Vol. IV, pp. 790–
793):

"I was asked, yes, at that point in time I was asked to, if I would
go into the room. And I did. I didn't agree with some of the statements
that were being made to support the decision. I was never asked nor
polled, and it was clearly a management decision from that point."

On prior warning (Boisjoly's July 31, 1985 internal memo to R. K. Lund):

"This letter is written to insure that management is fully aware of the
seriousness of the current O-Ring erosion problem... The result would
be a catastrophe of the highest order—loss of human life."

SOURCE 5 — NASA Marshall Space Flight Center internal documentation
referenced in Rogers Commission exhibits, specifically the January 27,
1986 FAX transmission from Thiokol listing concerns and the subsequent
signed launch recommendation after management caucus.
URL: https://history.nasa.gov/rogersrep/v1ch5.htm
==============================================================================

The signed Joe Kilminster FAX to Marshall, post-caucus, late January 27,
1986, contained the conclusion:

"MTI recommends STS-51L launch proceed on 28 January 1986. SRM-25 will
not be significantly different from SRM-15."

This superseded the Boisjoly/Thompson engineering recommendation of
"Do NOT launch below 53°F" which had been the Thiokol position at the
start of the teleconference.
