# Composites and game theory

## PNO — Persistent Non-Optimality (L6)

JSON count: **7** composite patterns.

| ID | Name | Definition (brief) |
|----|------|----------------------|
| `PNO-001` | Informational Persistent Non-Optimality | A persistent regime where decisions operate on distorted, filtered, or over-compressed representations of reality. |
| `PNO-002` | Incentive Persistent Non-Optimality | A persistent regime where declared institutional objectives diverge from effective actor-level objectives. |
| `PNO-003` | Aggregation Persistent Non-Optimality | A persistent regime where collective aggregation rules convert local preferences into unstable or non-optimal outcomes. |
| `PNO-004` | Coordination Persistent Non-Optimality | A persistent regime where known improvements are not executed because coordination costs, vetoes, or responsibility diffusion dominate. |
| `PNO-005` | Temporal Persistent Non-Optimality | A persistent regime where locally rational short-horizon decisions compound into long-horizon deterioration. |
| `PNO-006` | Inter-System Persistent Non-Optimality | A persistent regime where multiple institutional systems or jurisdictions optimize locally and create global mismatch or spillover. |
| `PNO-007` | Anti-Consensus Persistent Non-Optimality | A persistent regime where interaction among agents prevents convergence to a shared decision state or produces false/staged consensus. |

| `GT-013` | Auction / Bidding Failure | Procurement or allocation mechanism induces strategic bidding, winner's curse, collusion, or inefficient allocation. |
| `GT-014` | Matching Market Failure | Agents and positions/resources are mismatched because of preference revelation, constraints, or allocation rule defects. |
| `GT-015` | Evolutionary Strategy Lock-In | A strategy persists because it is locally stable, even when globally inferior alternatives exist. |
